Sleep Hygiene and Quality

The National Sleep Foundation recommends 7-9 hours of sleep for adults.

Sleep quality is as important as duration. Key markers:
- Fall asleep within 20 minutes of getting into bed
- Wake up no more than once during the night
- Feel refreshed upon waking

Sleep and health connections:
- Poor sleep increases cortisol (stress hormone), promoting fat storage
- Chronic sleep deprivation increases diabetes risk by 40%
- Even one night of poor sleep can reduce insulin sensitivity
- Sleep quality directly affects workout recovery and muscle growth

Sleep hygiene tips:
- Maintain consistent sleep/wake times (even on weekends)
- Keep bedroom cool (18-20°C), dark, and quiet
- Avoid screens 30-60 minutes before bed (blue light disrupts melatonin)
- Limit caffeine after 2 PM and alcohol within 3 hours of bedtime
- Consider a wind-down routine: stretching, reading, or deep breathing

Sleep and exercise relationship:
- Regular exercise improves sleep quality, but avoid intense workouts within 2 hours of bedtime
- Morning sunlight exposure helps regulate circadian rhythm
- Naps: limit to 20-30 minutes before 3 PM

Sleep disorders to watch for:
- Three or more nights of insomnia per week for over 3 months: consult a doctor
- Loud snoring with pauses: possible sleep apnea (especially if BMI > 30)
- Restless legs: may indicate iron deficiency
