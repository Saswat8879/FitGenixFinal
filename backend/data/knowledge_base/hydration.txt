Hydration Guidelines

Proper hydration is essential for metabolism, digestion, joint lubrication, and temperature regulation.

How much water do you need?
- General formula: 35 mL per kg of body weight (e.g., 70 kg person needs ~2450 mL/day)
- Active individuals may need 500-1000 mL more on exercise days
- Hot climates or high altitude: increase intake by 20-30%

Signs of dehydration:
- Dark yellow urine (aim for pale straw color)
- Headaches, fatigue, dizziness
- Dry mouth and skin
- Decreased exercise performance

Hydration timing:
- Drink 200-300 mL within 30 minutes of waking
- Sip water throughout the day rather than drinking large amounts at once
- Drink 200-400 mL 30 minutes before exercise
- During exercise: 150-250 mL every 15-20 minutes
- After exercise: 500-700 mL per 0.5 kg of body weight lost

What counts toward hydration:
- Water (best choice)
- Herbal tea (caffeine-free)
- Water-rich fruits and vegetables (watermelon, cucumber, oranges)
- Caffeinated beverages count partially but have mild diuretic effects

Hydration and chronic conditions:
- Diabetes: adequate hydration helps kidneys flush excess glucose
- Hypertension: proper hydration helps maintain blood volume
- Kidney disease: follow your doctor's fluid intake guidelines (may be restricted)
