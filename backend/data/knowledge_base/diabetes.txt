Diabetes Prevention and Management

Type 2 diabetes is a chronic condition affecting how the body processes blood sugar (glucose). Key risk factors include obesity (BMI over 30), sedentary lifestyle, family history, age over 45, and poor diet.

Prevention strategies backed by evidence:
- Lose 5-7% of body weight if overweight (CDC Diabetes Prevention Program)
- 150 minutes per week of moderate exercise (brisk walking, cycling)
- Reduce intake of refined carbohydrates and added sugars
- Increase dietary fiber to at least 25g per day
- Monitor blood glucose levels regularly if pre-diabetic

For people already diagnosed with Type 2 diabetes:
- Follow a consistent carbohydrate intake plan
- Aim for HbA1c below 7% (per ADA guidelines)
- Check blood sugar before and after meals
- Take prescribed medication on schedule
- Exercise helps improve insulin sensitivity — even 10-minute walks after meals

Glycemic index matters: choose low-GI foods like whole grains, legumes, and non-starchy vegetables. Avoid sugary beverages and processed snacks.

Pre-diabetes warning signs: fasting glucose 100-125 mg/dL, HbA1c 5.7-6.4%. Lifestyle changes at this stage can prevent or delay Type 2 diabetes by 58% (DPP study).
